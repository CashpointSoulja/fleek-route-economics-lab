# ELI5: how the calculator works

Imagine buying a big sack of second-hand jackets abroad and selling them to shops in the UK.

1. **The sack.** It weighs, say, 1,000 kg. Each jacket weighs about 0.5 kg, so there are 2,000 jackets.
2. **Not all are good.** Graders keep 70% (1,400). The rest is "rag" sold cheaply by the kilo.
3. **Not all good ones sell in time.** 90% of the good ones sell (1,260). Of those, 4% come back as returns, so buyers keep 1,210. Kept ÷ bought = **saleable yield** (60.5%).
4. **What it cost.** The sack's price is in the seller's currency. To get pounds you divide by the exchange rate (how many of their units one pound buys). If the pound weakens, you get fewer of their units per pound, so the sack costs more pounds.
5. **Getting it here.** Freight (a fixed fee plus a price per kg), insurance, then duty (a percentage of goods + freight + insurance) and import VAT (a percentage of that plus duty). A customs broker / importer of record charges a fee.
6. **VAT is special.** If the importer can reclaim import VAT, it is not a cost, but you still pay it on arrival and only get it back weeks later. So it ties up cash without reducing profit. If it cannot be reclaimed, it is a cost.
7. **Getting it to buyers.** Handling in the UK, parcels to buyers (per order), card fees, return handling.
8. **What's left** after all of that is the **contribution**. Divide by kept jackets for **contribution per unit**, or by revenue for **margin**.
9. **Cash.** You pay for the sack and freight on day 0, duty and VAT when it lands, and buyers' money arrives over the selling weeks. The lowest point of your bank balance is the **cash tied up**.
10. **Stress.** What if freight is 25% dearer, the pound 10% weaker, or 3 more jackets in every 100 come back? If any one of those pushes margin under your floor, the answer is "hold", not "go".
11. **Gates.** If nobody knows the duty rate, or nobody has agreed to be the importer of record, or a key number has no dated source, the answer is "blocked", even if the maths looks great. A good margin built on an unknown is not a good margin.
12. **Getting to £5M.** £5M ÷ price per jacket = jackets buyers must keep. Add returns to get jackets sold, divide by jackets per order to get orders, divide £5M by one sack's net sales to get sacks per year. Nothing here says buyers want that many; it only says what it would take.
